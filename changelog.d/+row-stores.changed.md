One thread assembles a full list's rows from dword permutes of two registers, the eight compressed targets with the source and both shifts and the eight compressed distances, so the second four hits of a vector need no further shuffles; the 4096-atom call drops from 1.45 to 1.39 ms. When bins hold under eight atoms on average, the block loop is compiled with the tile's target features and the tile inlines into it, for rows and for columns: 256 atoms in the 18 Å cube take 0.0167 ms from Rust and 0.0224 ms from Python, against 0.038 and 0.056 ms for 0.3.8. On Linux (x86_64 and aarch64) the `Pair` vector and the columns of at least 4 MB that a cutoff call allocates get `MADV_HUGEPAGE` on their 2 MB-aligned interior, so the row stores walk the page tables once per 2 MB; buffers a caller passes in are not touched, and the system's policy decides. With this host's transparent huge pages in madvise mode the one-thread call drops from 1.47 to 1.40 ms, and without the advice it takes 1.32 ms after the row prefetch.
