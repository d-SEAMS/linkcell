`cell_for_pbc` and `pairs_within_pbc` bin every open axis from the periodic sublattice. The open row is replaced by a unit normal to the span of the periodic rows, whatever length the caller stored. A near-flat open row no longer collapses the periodic face widths, so a slab of lengths 13, 13, 23.3 Å with angles 86.892, 16.892, 70 degrees and `pbc` TTF returns the minimum-image pairs instead of `TooManyCells`. The same frame with TFT, FTT, and TFF follows the periodic axes that remain. A fully periodic cell whose shortest face altitude is at most `1e-6` of its longest edge returns `Error::Degenerate` (`periodic cell is degenerate: a face width has collapsed`) from `cell_for_pbc`, `pairs_within`, `pairs_within_pbc`, `pairs_within_csr`, and the k-nearest entries, instead of `TooManyImages` or a row-buffer overflow. The C and Python pair entries use the same lattice.
