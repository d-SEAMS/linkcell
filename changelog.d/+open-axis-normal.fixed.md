`cell_for_pbc` and `pairs_within_pbc` bin every open axis from the periodic sublattice. The open row is replaced by a unit normal to the span of the periodic rows, whatever length the caller stored. A near-flat open row no longer collapses the periodic face widths, so a slab of lengths 13, 13, 23.3 Å with angles 86.892, 16.892, 70 degrees and `pbc` TTF returns the minimum-image pairs instead of `TooManyCells`. The same frame with TFT, FTT, and TFF follows the periodic axes that remain. `Error::Degenerate` (`periodic cell is degenerate: a face width is zero or non-finite`) is only a periodic face width that is zero or non-finite. A positive finite width is not rejected by its ratio to the longest edge. The image count (`2 * ceil(cutoff / width) + 1`, product at most 2^24) depends on the cutoff. The mesh edge is clamped to the shortest width, so a face much shorter than the other edges can exceed the bin cap at every cutoff; that refusal is `TooManyCells`, not `Degenerate`. When both caps hold, including a short face no shorter than the cutoff, the search returns the pairs. Those caps, and a checked product of the estimated row count with the row size, run before any buffer is reserved. A reservation that does not fit, or that the allocator refuses, is `TooManyImages` and does not abort. The partner stencil is capped at 2^31 slots, enough for a reach of two bins on every axis of a mesh at the bin cap, and is not stored past that. A gather whose step buffer the allocator refuses is `TooManyImages` and does not abort. A cell of widths 10, 10 and 1.1e-5 at cutoff 10 stays under the image cap and returns `TooManyCells` from the bin cap before any row buffer is reserved. The C and Python pair entries use the same lattice.
