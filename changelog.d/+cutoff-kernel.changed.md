The cutoff tile forms `|r_q|^2 - 2 r_q . p' + |p'|^2` from positions relative to the bin corner, with three fused multiply-adds per lane. A lane within a rounding bound of the cutoff squared is decided by the direct formula, so the rows do not change; `dist2` agrees with the direct value to within that bound (about 2e-10 Å² on an 18 Å cube). One thread writes a full list straight from the tile. The bins are a fold, a per-bin count, a scan, and a scatter into slot columns; the fold takes eight points at a time on AVX-512 and gives the same bits as `Cell::fractional` and `Cell::cartesian`, for the k-nearest mesh as well. A walk that splits across threads (from ten thousand expected pairs) reserves its rows on the caller's thread, then searches and writes in one pool entry. On this host a periodic 18 Å cube, 4 Å cutoff, and 4096 atoms takes 1.61 ms on one thread and 0.33 ms on eight from Rust, against 2.30 and 0.86 ms for 0.3.8 over eleven interleaved rounds; 0.3.8's eight-thread time moves with the host's wake latency, and in one run it took 0.46 ms against 0.29 ms here. With the atoms shuffled the call takes 1.57 and 0.33 ms, against 2.86 and 0.95 ms for 0.3.8. Python takes 1.34 and 0.33 ms against 5.61 and 5.10 ms, C 1.68 and 0.32 ms against 4.42 and 3.98 ms, and C++ 2.85 and 2.59 ms against 9.85 and 8.56 ms. vesin 0.6.2's cell list takes 32 ms and 6 to 9 ms.
